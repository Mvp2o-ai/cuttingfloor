# Make a highlight reel

The techniques are their own skills: scoring-to-picture for music made for the cut, nat-pops for the voice through the music. This adds the questions, the order and the build that make it a reel.

## When to use
- Clips of an event, game, season, trip or product, and a request for a short reel with music.

## Prerequisites
- The cuttingfloor MCP server is connected.
- The clips are imported.

## Steps
1. The assistant should ask, in one message and only for what the user hasn't said: the length and where it will be posted (vertical for Shorts and Reels, wide for YouTube); who or what to feature, with names spelled; their song, or music made for it, and if made, whether they want to hum or tap a tune or send a sound to build it from; the words for the end card; anything to leave out. Then it works, filling any gap with its own choice.
2. The assistant should find the material before any music: the transcript and the shots, each key moment found and pinned to its frame (the ball through the net, the kiss, the reveal), and the climax chosen: the one moment everything builds to.
3. The assistant should plan the order as a build, not a timeline: a hook in the first two seconds (a strong moment or line, never the warm-up), then each moment better than the last, the climax, then the reaction and the end card. Each moment runs 1.5 to 4 seconds; faces and crowd reactions go in as one-beat flashes between them.
4. The assistant should make the music to that plan (scoring-to-picture), or find the beats and drop of the user's song, then cut every shot on a beat, put each key moment's peak on a beat, and slow the climax so its peak frame lands on the biggest hit.
5. The assistant should mix the voice in (nat-pops): the pops on their moments, filler out, the music ducked under each pop and nothing over the hit.
6. The assistant should mark each key moment with a graphic (the name, what happened) and a sound effect, both starting on the moment's frame, in one style, clear of faces and the action; the reel ends on the end card over the reaction. Only the key moments get a graphic.
7. The assistant should check the export before handing it over (the QA skill): loudness and peaks, every pop read back from its transcript, the biggest hit on the climax frame, names as the user spelled them.
8. The assistant should make later requests ("add the names", "darker music", "more flashes") as changes to the same timeline and score, keeping the rest frame for frame.

## Decision table
| Situation | Do |
|---|---|
| The user sends a song | Cut to its beats, with its drop on the climax frame; slide the song's start to make it land |
| No voice in the footage | Natural-sound pops only (the hit, the cheer), or music only |
| A sizzle or company reel with interviews | The pops are the strongest sentences over b-roll; the music thinner under them |
| One person featured (a recruiting reel) | Only their best moments, their name and number on the first, a frame that shows which one they are |
| Vertical asked of wide footage | pan-and-scan, shot by shot |
| Far more footage than reel | Moments and climax first; music only once their frames are known |

## Required behavior
- The user's questions come in one message, then the work goes ahead.
- Every cut is on a beat and the climax lands on the biggest hit, checked on the export.
- The best lines are heard and the filler is gone.
- Changes edit the same timeline and score.
